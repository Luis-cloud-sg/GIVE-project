Comparison between VLA policies and GIVE. General VLA policies struggle to accurately ground target objects and interpret human intent from hand gestures, whereas GIVE incorporates visual-semantic guidance that improves intent comprehension and enhances HRI performance.

Current VLA models treat robotic manipulation as a purely language-driven task, struggling when human intentions are conveyed through non-verbal gestures.

We propose GIVE, an effective approach that enhances pre-trained VLA models with human gesture understanding without architectural modifications. By incorporating gesture information through two complementary pathways—a visual pathway for object grounding and a semantic pathway for robust intent grounding—GIVE enables VLA policies to accurately associate gestures with manipulation behaviors.

Methodology

GIVE for VLA models in HRI scenarios. GIVE transforms human gestures into two complementary guidance pathways: visual gesture prompting provides spatial grounding of manipulation targets, while semantic intent parsing captures high-level intent description. These cues are fed into a VLA model to generate continuous actions.

Quantitative Results & Ablation

Overall Execution Performance

We evaluate the end-to-end performance in a sequential grasp-then-handover HRI task. The baseline π0.5 struggles without explicit visual guidance, achieving only a 46.7% Identify Success Rate (SR) and a 0% Handover SR. In contrast, GIVE significantly improves early-stage grounding (86.7% Identify SR) and consistently maintains an 80.0% success rate across all subsequent execution stages (Grasp, React, and Handover).

Ablation: Visual and Semantic Guidance Components

We further study the role of each design component by comparing visual-only and full visual-semantic guidance. Purely keypoint-based geometric prompting provides only limited benefits. Explicitly introducing fingertip-directed rays leads to a substantial improvement in early-stage grounding. However, visual guidance alone remains insufficient for handling temporal state transitions (achieving only 23.3% React SR). In contrast, the full model combining both visual and semantic priors provides complementary improvements.

Ablation: Gesture Keypoint Integration Strategies

To understand how best to incorporate gesture cues into the policy learning, we compared our parameter-free Visual Overlay against two token-based injection methods.

As shown above, the direct visual overlay achieves the best performance. Token-based variants introduce additional encoding modules that can disrupt pretrained visual-language representations, whereas our visual overlay preserves them while explicitly encoding the spatial relation between the pointing gesture and candidate objects.

Robustness & Generalization

Unseen Spatial Layouts & Objects

We evaluate spatial robustness by testing three different target objects (peach, mango, carrot) across three distinct workspace locations. The baseline model exhibits significant performance degradation and high variance, indicating sensitivity to spatial distribution shifts. Conversely, GIVE maintains high and stable success rates across all challenging setups.

Generalization to Unseen Human Participants

We further test the framework on completely unseen participants featuring hand sizes, body shapes, and clothing styles distinct from the training distribution. The baseline's identification capability deteriorates significantly. In contrast, GIVE achieves consistent identification success, demonstrating that our explicit hand skeleton visual guidance successfully decouples intent reasoning from visual appearance variations.
